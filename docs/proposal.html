Terrasync: An Ambient Focus Terrarium


Project Concept / Motivation

One way I maintain motivation while working is by cultivating a clean and visually engaging environment. I have found that surrounding myself with meaningful objects such as small trinkets, warm lighting, and photographs of friends and family positively influences my focus and overall mindset. However, I wanted to design something that extends beyond aesthetics and serves a functional and motivational purpose. Terrasync emerged from this intention to create a physical object that not only enhances the visual quality of a workspace but also actively supports focus and environmental awareness. In addition, this project provides an opportunity to further develop my Unity skills and strengthen my technical fluency in interactive systems.


Project Description

Terrasync is a small dome-shaped ambient desk lamp that integrates environmental sensing with a synchronized digital ecosystem in Unity. The physical form will resemble a compact terrarium dome, designed as a cohesive and intentional desktop object rather than an exposed electronics prototype. Inside, RGB LEDs will create a soft, diffused glow, while a photoresistor embedded on the side of the base will detect ambient light levels in the surrounding environment. The LED brightness will dynamically respond to room lighting, allowing the lamp to adapt naturally to its context. Through serial communication, the Arduino will transmit environmental data to a Unity application running as a small widget on the user’s laptop. The Unity interface will display a terrarium-like simulation that transitions between daytime and nighttime states based either on the real-world time of day or the brightness detected by the photoresistor, creating a synchronized relationship between the physical environment and the digital ecosystem.

In addition to environmental responsiveness, Terrasync introduces a focus interaction through a side-mounted button integrated into the enclosure. A single press activates Focus Mode, prompting a digital plant within the Unity terrarium to begin growing. Pressing the button again initiates a pause request, which is clearly displayed in the Unity widget to prevent accidental interruption. A second confirming press finalizes the pause and causes the growing plant to wither, visually representing a break in focus. This layered interaction should discourage unintentional disengagement while preserving user control. By combining ambient light adaptation with a tangible focus mechanism, Terrasync reframes productivity as something cultivated within an ecosystem rather than measured solely through timers.


Anticipated Bill of Materials

The physical prototype will include an Arduino Uno, RGB LEDs, a photoresistor for light sensing, a push button for interaction, appropriate resistors for current limiting and voltage division, a breadboard or perfboard for circuit assembly, and wires. A USB cable will provide power and enable serial communication between the Arduino and the Unity application. The enclosure will be 3D printed, consisting of a translucent dome to diffuse the LED light and an opaque base to house the electronics. The button and photoresistor will be embedded into the side of the base to maintain a clean, integrated product form.

On the software side, Unity will be used to develop a lightweight desktop widget that visualizes the terrarium simulation. C# scripts will manage serial communication, environmental state mapping, and focus mode logic. The Unity interface will remain compact and peripheral, reinforcing the system’s role as calm, ambient technology rather than a disruptive productivity tool.


Timeline and Contingency Plans

In Week 1, I will construct the hardware system, including the LED circuit, photoresistor voltage divider, and button input. I will establish reliable serial communication between Arduino and Unity, ensuring that light values and button states are correctly transmitted and received. The milestone for this week will be a Unity scene that responds in real time to sensor input and button presses. If serial communication becomes unstable, I will simulate environmental values within Unity while debugging hardware independently.

In Week 2, I will develop the terrarium simulation in Unity, implementing day and night transitions based on either system time or live photoresistor data. I will also implement the focus state, including Idle, Active Focus, Pause Requested, and Paused states. Growth and withering animations will be tied directly to these states, and LED brightness will be mapped to environmental readings to maintain synchronization between the physical and digital systems. If real-time mapping proves too sensitive, I will implement threshold-based lighting transitions to maintain stability.

In Week 3, I will finalize the 3D-printed enclosure, integrate and secure all internal components, refine light diffusion for a soft ambient glow, and polish the Unity interface for visual clarity and subtlety. The final milestone will be a fully integrated prototype demonstrating cohesive hardware input, environmental sensing, serial communication, and responsive digital visualization. If fabrication challenges arise, I will modify the enclosure while preserving the functional and aesthetic integration of the system.
